Glass Disposal and Removal FAQ

What types of glass can you remove?

We handle the removal of all types of glass, including broken windows, glass doors, mirrors, shower enclosures, glass furniture, and glass panels. We also manage the safe disposal of larger glass fixtures such as tabletops and display cases. However, we do not remove glass that may contain hazardous materials, such as tempered glass with embedded wires.

Do you offer same-day or next-day service for glass removal?

Yes, we offer same-day or next-day services for glass removal, depending on availability. Since glass can be hazardous if broken or improperly handled, we recommend scheduling an appointment as soon as possible to ensure safe and quick removal.

How do you ensure safe removal and disposal of glass?

Safety is our top priority when handling glass removal. Our team wears protective gear, including gloves, safety glasses, and sturdy clothing, to prevent injuries. We carefully remove large glass pieces using specialized tools and techniques to prevent further breakage. Smaller shards are collected with brooms, vacuums, and adhesive pads. After removal, we ensure that the glass is disposed of responsibly, following local recycling guidelines when possible.

Do I need to prepare the glass for removal beforehand?

No, you don't need to prepare or move anything yourself. Our team will handle everything from carefully removing glass fixtures or broken shards to cleaning up after the job is done. Whether the glass is still intact or already shattered, we manage the entire process, ensuring your property is left safe and clean.

How do you prevent damage to surrounding areas during glass removal?

Our team uses padded protective gear, drop cloths, and careful handling techniques to avoid causing damage to the surrounding areas during glass removal. For large, fragile items like windows or doors, we use suction cups and lifting tools to safely extract them from their frames without scratching floors or walls. We also take extra care to remove small shards to prevent any future safety risks.
